Contractor Marketing Starts With Showing Your Work

A homeowner planning a renovation needs to understand your process, the projects you take on and what happens after they enquire. This guide helps you turn that information into a useful website and a clearer marketing brief.

Help Customers Decide Whether You Fit

Define the projects you want

Separate whole-home renovations, additions, tenant improvements and small repairs. Explain which work your team handles, where you operate and what information you need to assess a project. A visitor should understand your scope before contacting you.

Show the process behind the finish

Before-and-after photographs are a starting point. Explain the original problem, the scope agreed, access or occupancy constraints and the work completed. Credit the participating trades and publish only photographs and details you have permission to share.

Make enquiries useful

Ask for the city, property type, work needed, preferred timing and available drawings. Explain who will respond and the next step. Keep project enquiries separate from supplier offers and marketing pitches, and test the receiving mailbox.

Give Each Service a Clear Home

Organize your website around real services and project examples. A kitchen renovation page should explain your approach to an occupied home, trade coordination and finish choices. A tenant-improvement page should address access, phasing and commercial requirements. Link each service to relevant examples and the enquiry route.

Add local detail when it helps a customer understand how you work in that area. Use actual project locations with permission, rather than repeating the same generic page under different city names.

Turn One Project Into Useful Content

A practical project record

Record the property type and area, the customer’s starting problem, your scope, the steps taken and the outcome. Include useful constraints: working around occupants, coordinating trades or protecting finished spaces. Use those details to answer the questions a similar customer would ask.

A contribution to the industry

Share a focused article or short video explaining a lesson from that work. A useful contribution can cover planning, materials, coordination or maintenance. BuildersHaus welcomes relevant proposals through IAM; publication and any commercial terms are agreed separately.

Measure Enquiries Through to Jobs

Keep a simple record of the originating website, service requested, location, qualified enquiry, estimate and confirmed job. Search visibility, a referral and an awarded contract are different results. Reviewing those stages helps identify whether the next improvement belongs on the website or in follow-up.
